About

My research sits at the intersection of deep internal learning, test-time learning and self-supervised signal enhancement. The question I keep returning to is whether a network can learn everything it needs about a degradation from the one signal in front of it.

I developed an utterance-specific approach to speech dereverberation, studied how acoustic mismatch limits it, and evaluated the downstream effect on automatic speech recognition (ASR) using LibriSpeech. I am now extending the same idea to speech bandwidth extension and electrocardiogram (ECG) enhancement. More broadly I care about data-efficient machine learning and speech and audio systems that hold up outside the lab. Alongside the PhD I work part-time as an AI research associate at Omega Point Research, building multi-agent research tooling for investment workflows.

Research

One idea, three signals: learn the inverse of a degradation from the observation itself.

PhD research · October 2023 – present

Deep internal learning for speech dereverberation

Can a network reverse an unknown room acoustic from a single reverberant recording, with no clean reference, no training corpus and no pretrained model? I proposed a deep internal learning (DIL) approach that constructs a training task inside the utterance using an auxiliary acoustic degradation model, then trains a compact multi-scale convolutional network ≈0.21 M parameters from scratch on that one utterance.

  • A structured sensitivity analysis of how the mismatch between the training-time and true room impulse responses (RIRs) governs performance.
  • Benchmarks against classical unsupervised (WPE — weighted prediction error), pretrained discriminative (DeepFilterNet) and generative (BUDDy) methods on three speech corpora: gains under matched acoustics, and a clear map of where mismatch breaks down.
  • Downstream ASR evaluation on LibriSpeech with measured RIRs, asking when signal-level gains actually translate into fewer recognition errors.

Ongoing · 2026 – present

Deep internal learning for speech bandwidth extension

Whether frequency-dependent structure learned from the observed lower bands of a single utterance can be transferred upward to reconstruct high-frequency content it has never seen. I built convolutional internal frequency-extrapolation models and a hybrid spectral band replication (SBR) variant; early results beat a conventional SBR baseline on a small development set.

Ongoing · 2026 – present

Deep internal learning for ECG signal enhancement

Taking DIL out of speech. Two strategies: degradation-based DIL, which learns to remove additional synthetic noise, and repetition-based DIL, which exploits recurring cardiac structure through beat-aligned masked reconstruction with a 2-D convolutional network. Evaluated on waveform reconstruction, R-peak detection and the effect of heartbeat alignment.

MSc thesis · 2022 – 2023

Mitigating gender bias in text-to-image generation

An in-processing debiasing method for Stable Diffusion that uses sampling-based probability estimation to selectively augment under-represented gender prompts, cutting the average gender-bias ratio from 0.325 to 0.053 −83.7 % across profession, scenario and trait prompt groups.
